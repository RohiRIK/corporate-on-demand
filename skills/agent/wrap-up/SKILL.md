# Finishing a task

The last few minutes decide whether the work was useful.

## Before you finish

1. **Run it.** The thing you changed, for real, not the suite alone.
2. **Look at what you actually changed**: `git status` and `git diff`. A file you
   did not mean to touch is a finding, not a detail.
3. **Commit** it, on your branch, with a message saying what changed and why.
4. **Never push. Never merge.** You have no authority to land anything. That is
   the CEO's job and it is not yours to take.

## Reporting

Your report is the only description of your work anyone will get. It should let
a reader decide what happened without opening anything.

- Say what you changed, by file.
- Say how you know it works - the command and what it printed.
- **Say what you did not do, and why.** A partial result reported as complete is
  the worst thing you can send back, because it costs the company its trust in
  your next report.

## If it did not work

Say so plainly, with the error, and say what you ruled out. Do not report a
failure as a partial success, and do not report a success you did not observe.

Nobody is waiting for good news. They are waiting for the truth, which is the
only thing that makes the next report worth reading.
